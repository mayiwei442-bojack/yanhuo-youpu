L50: This Chinese Braised Chicken with Mushrooms is a simple, home-cooked recipe, using just a few ingredients. Serve with rice and leafy greens for a quick and simple dinner.
L51: Note: We first published this recipe in August 2013. We have since re-tested it, re-photographed it, and added metric measurements with clearer instructions.
L52: ## A Recipe Inspired by the Jade Temple
L53: 
L54: My mom came up with this Chinese Braised Chicken with Mushrooms recipe after visiting the Jade Temple (玉佛寺) in Shanghai.
L55: 
L56: The temple got its name from the two jade buddhas that “live” there, each carved from a complete piece of jade and “invited” from Myanmar in the late 1800s. If you find yourself in cite33†Shanghai†amzn.to , you have to go there.
L57: 
L58: Reason 1: You’ll get the experience of seeing a working buddhist temple.
L59: 
L60: Reason 2: Double mushroom noodles!
L61: After you pay a visit to both buddhas, stop at the Jade Temple’s kitchen, where they crank out cheap vegetarian dishes. They’re most well-known for their noodle soups.
L62: 
L63: Order a bowl of their Braised Double Mushroom Noodle Soup, and you won’t be sorry. Kaitlin gets weekly cravings for it. One summer, she got noodle fever, and made us all get on a 5-hour high speed train from Beijing to Shanghai for that bowl of noodle soup. That’s how good it is!
L64: Here is their address and phone number: 玉佛寺 / 上海 普陀区 江宁路999号/ 02162665596. Just copy it and give it to the taxi driver!
L65: 
L66: cite34†Image: Braised Chicken and Dried Wood Ear and Shiitake Mushroom L67: 
L68: Now, obviously this is not a vegetarian dish. But it does feature “double” mushrooms—dried shiitakes and dried wood ears—braised together in a rich, dark sauce.
L69: The chicken wings add a stickiness to that sauce that’s hard to resist. It’s perfect with some steamed rice. Just add a green vegetable, and dinner’s on the table!
L70:

L73: 
L74: Heat the 2 tablespoons oil in your wok over medium heat. Add the cite36†ginger and let it cook for about a minute until fragrant.
L75: 
L76: cite37†Image: Frying ginger slices in wok L77: 
L78: Then turn up the heat to high and add the chicken.
L79: 
L80: cite38†Image: Frying chicken wing pieces with oil and ginger in wok L81: 
L82: Keep stirring until the chicken is browned, and add the soaked cite16†dried shiitake mushrooms and cite39†wood ear mushrooms . Keep stirring for another couple minutes.
L83: cite40†Image: Adding shiitake and wood ear mushrooms to chicken L84: 
L85: Then add 1 to 1 ¼ cup of the water you used to soak the mushrooms.
L86: 
L87: cite41†Image: Adding braising liquid to mushrooms and chicken L88: 
L89: Also add the cite42†Shaoxing wine , sugar, cite14†soy sauce , and cite43†dark soy sauce . mix everything well.
L90: 
L91: cite44†Image: Stirring seasonings into chicken and mushrooms L92: 
L93: Bring the liquid to a boil, cover the wok, and simmer over low heat for about 40-45 minutes, stirring occasionally, or until the sauce is thick and gooey.
L94: cite45†Image: Reduced sauce in wok of chicken and Chinese mushrooms L95: 
L96: If the sauce is still too thin after 45 minutes, turn up the heat and reduce the sauce until it coats the chicken and mushrooms. You can also add more water if you like more sauce.
L97: 
L98: Stir in the cite21†scallions , and cook until they’re just wilted.
L99: 
L100: cite46†Image: Stirring scallions into chicken and mushrooms L101: 
L102: Serve your Chinese Braised Chicken with Mushrooms with lots of rice and a green vegetable!

L114: ### Chinese Braised Chicken with Mushrooms
L115: 
L116: Chinese Braised Chicken w/ Mushrooms is a dish rich in flavor and umami––perfect with a side of rice and a sauteed green vegetable.
L117: 
L118: by: Judy
L119: 
L120: Serves: 4
L121: 
L122: Prep: 20 mins
L123: 
L124: Cook: 45 mins
L125: 
L126: Total: 1 hr 5 mins
L127: #### Ingredients
L128:   * [Input]2 tablespoons oil (vegetable, canola, or any neutral flavored oil)
L129:   * [Input]3 slices cite36†ginger L130:   * [Input]1 1/3 lbs chicken wings (drummettes and flats)
L131:   * [Input]25 cite16†small dried shiitake mushrooms (about the size of a quarter, rinsed and soaked in 2 cups of warm water for 2 hours until soft. Reserve soaking liquid! Can substitute 10 large mushrooms)
L132:   * [Input]¾ cup cite39†dried wood ear mushrooms (rinsed and soaked 1 hour until reconstituted)
L133:   * [Input]1 1/4 cup water from soaking the mushrooms
L134:   * [Input]¼ cup cite55†Shaoxing wine L135:   * [Input]1 teaspoon sugar
L136:   * [Input]1 tablespoon cite56†soy sauce L137:   * [Input]2½ teaspoons cite57†dark soy sauce L138:   * [Input]1 cite21†scallion (sliced)
L139: [Button: US Customary][Button: Metric]
L140: #### Instructions
L141: 
L142:   * Heat the oil in your wok over medium heat. Add the ginger, and cook for 1 minute, or until fragrant and beginning to crisp at the edges. Increase the heat to high and add the chicken. Cook the chicken for 3-5 minutes, or until lightly golden.
L143: 
L144:   * Add the soaked shiitakes and wood ear mushrooms. Stir-fry for 2 minutes.
L145:   * Add the 1 1/4 cup of the water you used to soak the mushrooms. Also add the Shaoxing wine, sugar, light soy sauce, and dark soy sauce. Mix everything well, and bring the liquid to a boil.
L146: 
L147:   * Reduce the heat to low, cover the wok, and simmer for 40-45 minutes, or until most of the liquid has cooked off. Make sure to stir occasionally, and remember to keep the heat low (add additional water if it dries out too quickly).
L148: 
L149:   * Finally, stir in the scallions until they're wilted. Serve!

L163: About
L164: 
L165: Sarah
L166: Sarah Leung is the eldest daughter in The Woks of Life family, working alongside younger sister Kaitlin and parents Bill and Judy. You could say this multigenerational recipe blog was born out of two things: 1) her realization in college that she had no idea how to make her mom’s Braised Pork Belly and 2) that she couldn’t find a job after graduation. With the rest of the family on board, she laid the groundwork for the blog in 2013.
L167: By 2015, it had become one of the internet’s most trusted resources for Chinese cooking. Creator of quick and easy recipes for harried home cooks and official Woks of Life photographer, Sarah loves creating accessible recipes that chase down familiar nostalgic flavors while adapting to the needs of modern home cooks.
L168: Alongside her family, Sarah has become a New York Times Bestselling author with their cookbook, The Woks of Life: Recipes to Know and Love from a Chinese American Family, as well as a James Beard Award nominee and IACP Award finalist.
